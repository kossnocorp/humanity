# Subsistence and cultural transmission

Humanity draws on mobile foragers of the Pleistocene and early Holocene. It does not assign an Earth date or reproduce one archaeological culture. The procedural planet has its own geography, languages and traditions. Sources establish examples of what people did; numerical yields, learning probabilities and prerequisites are simulation assumptions, not archaeological measurements.

## Evidence informing the model

| Evidence / example | What it informs here |
|---|---|
| [Smithsonian: Katanda harpoon point](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/getting-food/katanda-bone-harpoon-point), Democratic Republic of Congo, approximately 90,000–80,000 years ago | Specialized freshwater fishing is substantial subsistence, not an invention that must wait for farming. |
| O'Connor, Ono & Clarkson (2011), [Pelagic Fishing at 42,000 Years Before the Present and the Maritime Skills of Modern Humans](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1207703), Timor | Coastal and offshore resources support different strategies; tools and maritime skill affect access. The game restricts travel to short crossings and river legs, rather than reconstructing these voyages. |
| Hardy et al. (2020), [Direct evidence of Neanderthal fibre technology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-61839-w), Abri du Maras, France | Cordage is an old, sophisticated, perishable technology. Fiber knowledge supports nets, binding, carrying and watercraft. A preserved cord does not demonstrate all those applications at that site. |
| Nadel et al. (2012), [Processing wild cereal grains at Ohalo II](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003598X00048201), approximately 23,000 years ago | Hunter-fisher-gatherers processed wild cereals. Root, seed and nut processing improves foraging without turning it into agriculture. |
| Smithsonian: [making clothing](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/making-clothing), [carrying and storing](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior/carrying-storing), and [behavior](https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/behavior) | Hide working, sewing, containers and information sharing belong alongside hunting tools. Cold adaptations and storage have ecological consequences. Pottery is not a universal prerequisite for storage. |
| Migliano et al. (2020), [Hunter-gatherer multilevel sociality accelerates cumulative cultural evolution](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aax5913) | Inter-band networks can combine traditions. This study of contemporary networks and cultural-evolution modelling informs a mechanism; it does not measure prehistoric languages or teaching rates. |

These examples span different places, populations and periods. Living forager societies are not treated as unchanged replicas of prehistoric ones. Named archaeological assemblages such as the Gravettian or Magdalenian are not imposed as languages, ethnic identities or a mandatory progression. Instead, cold-country clothing traditions, woodland trapping, open-country cooperative hunting, river fishing, and coastal boat use can coexist and mix.

## Ecology and subsistence

- **Six land-animal analogues:** reindeer, bison, antelope, deer, boar and ibex. Temperature, woodland/highland preferences, group sizes, grazing demand, reproduction, hunting difficulty and meat yield differ. They compete for grazing, migrate and can be depleted by hunting. Small game remains a separate edible stock used by hunters/trappers.
- **Aquatic guilds:** freshwater fish in rivers/lakes; coastal and lower-density pelagic fish; shellfish in shallow seas and freshwater habitats. These are edible biomass in person-days, not individual counts. Temperature changes carrying capacity; ice and precipitation reduce harvest access. Harvest removes actual stocks. Renewal includes implicit recruitment/recolonization and can recover from zero.
- **Time allocation:** aquatic foraging takes effort away from terrestrial gathering and hunting. A small material-working budget is reserved. Shellfish need little specialized knowledge; fishing improves with fishing knowledge and fiber gear. Adjacent waters represent shoreline catchments, not literal daily trips to every neighboring cell center.
- **Materials:** renewable wood and fiber; geographically clustered toolstone; hides from hunts. Materials are portable, limited, used or worn down, and redistributed in peaceful encounters. Toolstone represents accessible geological supply, not an exhaustible quarry.
- **Water travel:** boats require wood, fiber, labor, knowledge and passenger places for the whole band. They wear down. Adjacent connected river legs reduce travel effort. Short crossings pass through one water cell between land camps; physical range depends on navigation and travel credit. Ice, storms and insufficient capacity block departures. Deep-water crossings need stronger navigation. Mishaps cost provisions/health. Camps remain on land.
- **Food handling and care:** plant processing changes gathering productivity; preservation reduces spoilage and increases stores, with wood improving preservation; cordage increases carrying capacity; hides and clothing reduce cold energy costs; care/remedy knowledge modestly improves recovery when food is sufficient.

## Knowledge lives in populations

Thirteen traditions now track **individual proficiency and practice experience**,
with derived child/adult/elder band means. Each human has a stable ID, actual birth
date, personal health, role and biography; rendering remains group-based.

Practice follows actual assigned work, including reduced learning from failed
attempts. Workers teach apprentices below 80% of their own proficiency, spending
time on instruction. Accounts of actual activities teach other members more slowly,
below 35% of the source's proficiency. Practice can exceed these social-learning
ceilings. These percentages are inspectable prototype assumptions, not measured
prehistoric teaching rates. Newborns begin untrained; aging transfers no averaged
skill between people. Local experiments seed one person's knowledge when conditions
permit. Losing an expert removes their personal skills from the band's capabilities.

Friendly encounters transfer one randomly selected topic from the better-informed band to the other. The chance is `0.04 + 0.66 × language overlap`, with stronger uptake at higher understanding. The nonzero floor represents demonstration and partial communication. Teachers retain knowledge. Contacts also change language mixtures, share provisions/materials, and spread species-specific hunting familiarity. Conflict interrupts these opportunities.

Traditions: stone working, cordage/baskets, fishing, watercraft, tracking, cooperative hunting, trapping, plant processing, preservation, clothing, care/remedies, oral teaching and water navigation. Hunting familiarity improves through successful and failed hunts. Starting repertoires vary by band and ecology, not presumed innate differences between language families.

## Residential size and camp fission

**A residential camp is not an entire language community or marriage network.** A few dozen co-residents are a useful baseline for mobile foragers, with substantial ecological and seasonal variation. There is no universal biological maximum of 50 or 150 people.

- [Ember, Heitmann & Wang Gaouette (2025), HRAF, *Hunter-Gatherers (Foragers)*](https://hraf.yale.edu/ehc/summaries/hunter-gatherers) summarizes mobile camps averaging about four families / 20 people (drawing on Hamilton et al. 2007). It also documents larger, sedentary or seasonally sedentary fishing communities, including New Guinea settlements exceeding 1,000. Thus 1,000 is not impossible for all foragers, but is inappropriate as an ordinary mobile residential band in this prototype.
- [Hill et al. (2011), *Co-Residence Patterns in Hunter-Gatherer Societies Show Unique Human Social Structure*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199071) samples 32 foraging societies, 5,067 people, and reports mean **experienced band size of 28.2 adults**. This is an adult-only, experienced-size measure, not an all-age mean of 28 people. Flexible residence and unrelated co-residents caution against modelling every band as one immutable extended family.
- [Hamilton et al. (2007), *The complex structure of hunter-gatherer social networks*](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.0564) distinguishes nested household, residential and wider social scales. Shared culture can persist across many separate camps.
- [Hamilton, Buchanan & Walker (2018), *Scaling the Size, Structure, and Dynamics of Residentially Mobile Hunter-Gatherer Camps*](https://doi.org/10.1017/aaq.2018.39), and HRAF's synthesis, inform flexible camp organization and increased spacing in larger camps. These ethnographic analogies do not establish exact prehistoric census sizes or splitting rules.

The simulation has three **invented, inherited residential customs**, not named Earth cultures. All numbers below are explicit modelling choices, informed by the scale of camps rather than measured cultural laws:

| Custom | Normal pressure threshold (all ages) | Response to sustained crowding |
|---|---:|---|
| Small-camp dispersal | 40 | After 14 pressure days, a small party (half the threshold) leaves |
| Balanced camp fission | 70 | After 28 pressure days, split into approximately equal camps |
| River-camp budding | 100 | After 45 pressure days, a smaller offshoot leaves while the larger parent remains |

River budding tolerates 150 only with fishing proficiency ≥30%, preservation ≥25%, ≥8 stored food-days/person, and accessible non-ocean aquatic biomass ≥30 food-days/person. Low stores (<3 days/person) or health (<65%) lower thresholds by a quarter. Pressure recedes by two days/day below the threshold. Camps above twice their current threshold split immediately and repeatedly as necessary; this also resolves oversized old-save bands on the next simulated day. This is a mobile-camp safeguard, not a claim that settlements or gatherings cannot be larger.

New camps begin at roughly half to five-sixths of their customary threshold, independent of global population. Waterside habitats bias, but do not dictate, river-budding customs. Splinters inherit customs and language mixtures, while actual individuals carry their own skills into the new band. Shared provisions and equipment are apportioned conservatively. Parent band IDs and individual identities persist; population, births and deaths do not change through fission. Two camps initially occupy the same coarse map cell and subsequently migrate with ordinary travel costs.

Individual people now transfer between bands at fission, preserving IDs, birth
dates, skills and histories. Sex/age-balanced selection still approximates a mixed
residential party; explicit households and complete pedigrees remain future work.
Residential customs transmit at fission and cultural identity change. Temporary
camp fusion and large seasonal assemblies remain future mechanics.

## Cultural identities and remembered places

The five starting variants and every residential/social tuning parameter live in [`humanity-cultures`](pkgs/humanity-cultures/README.md). A **variant** is a subsistence/residence strategy; a **culture identity** is a named, historically connected group of bands. They are distinct from language families. Isolation creates one-parent branches; repeated friendly contact can create two-parent mixed identities. The 3-year isolation and 60-contact-day mixing thresholds compress cultural change for observation and are not measured ethnographic identity-change rates.

Fishing-resident cultures establish remembered fishing settlements; seasonal-round cultures maintain camps associated with local seasons. This draws on HRAF's account of sedentary and seasonally sedentary fishing societies and [Robinson (2007), *Hunter-fisher-gatherer territoriality*, Internet Archaeology 22](https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue22/4/2_1.html). Robinson discusses Jordan's Khanty ethnography: multi-household spring/summer riverside bases, dispersed autumn/winter forest residences, and procurement locales inherited across generations. These contemporary/historical analogies illustrate possible mechanisms, not a universal prehistoric seasonal calendar or a claim that those societies were unchanged since the Pleistocene.

After 20 residence days with sufficient provisions and health, eligible bands remember a site. Fishing sites additionally require real aquatic stocks and fishing knowledge. Each culture/cell has one place with multiple seasons of use. Function (fishing/residential) is separate from observed occupation: season-specific days/person-days, occupation episodes and uninterrupted stays distinguish repeated reuse from long residence. Multiple bands may inhabit the same location; a cell still represents a territory, not a surveyed individual campsite.

The distinction follows [Clark et al. (2025), occupation intensity and hunter-gatherer settlement systems](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0333870): group size, stay duration and reoccupation frequency are different components of use. Multi-season reuse alone is not evidence of permanence. [Venkataraman et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1617542114) motivates future foraging-return/depletion improvements; these camp changes do not yet introduce sub-cell foraging trips or marginal-return-based movement.

Unmaintained place condition decays over approximately 3 years for residential camps and 6 for fishing locations. Healthy, provisioned associated residents repair condition; an unsupported visit does not magically restore it. These rates represent usable/remembered places, not empirically calibrated building or archaeological-site decay. Condition scales return attachment; a depleted place disappears and can later be re-established. Places also disappear when neither founder nor owning culture survives. No buildings, exclusive territorial rights, site storage, individual households or automatic band fusion are inferred from a marker.

## Inspection and boundaries

Select a cell and **scroll field notes** for stocks, wildlife species, carried materials, boat capacity, languages, cohort proficiencies and prey familiarity. `Tab` cycles biome, temperature, land food, aquatic biomass and craft materials. Materials map stone to red, wood to green and fiber to blue. Cyan fish symbols mark productive aquatic cells at close zoom.

Snapshots contain complete knowledge, equipment and ecology. Summaries report species populations, aquatic biomass, adult-weighted proficiency, harvest, innovations, transfers and boat journeys (`water_crossings`, including river legs). Journal knowledge events encode the topic with its stable `Skill::ALL` index.

This increment models practical subsistence traditions, not every aspect of prehistoric ritual, art, kinship, marriage, political institutions or every animal taxon. Craft types are functional aggregates; fish movement is implicit in recruitment; boats have capacity rather than individual entities. Water mishaps affect health; subsequent deaths use the existing nutrition/poor-health category.
